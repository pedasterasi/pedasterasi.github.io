Learning to finish small things

A tiny finished project teaches me more than a huge project I only imagine.

Starting is easy when an idea feels exciting. The harder part is staying with it once it becomes ordinary work: moving a button, fixing a small bug, or deciding when a detail is good enough.

I’m trying to make smaller promises to myself. One room instead of a whole world. One good interaction instead of ten systems. Something I can actually show at the end of a week.

Small is not the same as unimportant

A short project still has a beginning, a middle, and an ending. It lets me experience the full loop: an idea becomes a thing, someone can try it, and I can see what to improve next time.

Finishing does not mean perfect. It only means giving an idea the chance to become real.
