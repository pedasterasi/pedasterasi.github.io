Starting somewhere: why I’m making games in public.

The first note in a learning journey about Unity, small playable worlds, and allowing the work to be unfinished while it becomes something real.

Filed under

Learning in public

Status

In progress

Every game starts in a slightly awkward place: a folder with too many test scenes, a character that slides when it should walk, and an idea that is clearer in feeling than in words. This is mine.

I’m starting Pedas Terasi as a record of learning game development from the inside. Not a polished after-action report, but the small decisions, useful references, mistakes, and playable experiments that make up the work.

I want to make things small enough to finish, strange enough to remember, and honest enough to learn from.

Why keep a devlog?

Games are made from thousands of tiny choices. It is easy to lose sight of them while moving from one task to the next. Writing them down gives each experiment a little more shape: what I was trying to do, what changed, and what I want to test next.

It also makes the journey less lonely. If you are learning too, I hope there is something useful here—whether that is a solution, a question worth borrowing, or simply proof that everyone begins with a rough prototype.

A place for playable work

When there is a build worth sharing, it will live right inside the devlog. That means each note can carry a little piece of the project with it, not just screenshots and descriptions.

What I’m learning first

  • Player feel: movement that feels responsive before it looks impressive.
  • Useful feedback: small sounds, motion, and visual responses that tell the player they changed something.
  • Finishing loops: building one complete, tiny interaction rather than a giant promise.

For now, the goal is simple: make the next version a little more playable than the last one. I’ll share the result here when it is ready. Thanks for being here at the beginning.
